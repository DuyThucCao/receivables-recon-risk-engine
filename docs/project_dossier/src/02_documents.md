# Document dictionary

The pipeline reads only seven flat files. Each was reverse-engineered from a real-world document
type, then generated by a seeded simulator with realistic defects. Files are wide (header fields
repeated on every line), dates come in several text formats and identifiers are inconsistent, as
in real exports. Row counts are from the current build; every row is accounted for as either
staged or rejected with a reason.

| File | One row is | Reverse-engineered from | Raw rows |
|---|---|---|---:|
| `claims_export.csv` | one claim service line, claim header repeated | CMS-1500 / UB-04 claim forms | <!--n:recon.raw_rows.claims-->72,336<!--/n--> |
| `remittance_advice.csv` | one adjudicated service line on a remittance | 835 remittance advice / EOB | <!--n:recon.raw_rows.remits-->89,051<!--/n--> |
| `bank_statement.csv` | one bank transaction | operating-account statement export | <!--n:recon.raw_rows.bank-->1,831<!--/n--> |

| `drug_purchase_invoices.csv` | one invoice line: drug, lot, expiry, quantity, price | wholesaler invoice | <!--n:recon.raw_rows.invoices-->1,292<!--/n--> |

## Claims export (`claims_export.csv`)

**Real-world source.** The CMS-1500 (professional) and UB-04 (institutional) claim forms, exported
from a billing system with the claim header repeated on each service line.

**Key fields.** `claim_id` (hospital claim number, the patient control number the payer should echo),
`line_no`, `line_item_control_no` (claim id plus line number; some payers echo it), `payer_id`,
`member_id`, `procedure_code` (HCPCS/CPT; J-codes for drugs), `units`, `line_charge`,
`service_date`, `submission_date`, `revenue_code`, diagnosis codes.

**Messiness planted.** Payer codes in lower case or padded; charges written as `1234.50` or
`$1,234.50`; dates as MM/DD/YYYY or YYYYMMDD; blank procedure codes; repeated rows. Lines that
cannot be typed go to `stg.reject` with reasons such as `MISSING_PROCEDURE_CODE` or
`UNPARSEABLE_AMOUNT`.

**Becomes.** `core.fact_claim_line` (grain: one claim line), `core.dim_procedure`,
`core.dim_service_line`; the claim header is the view `core.v_claim`.

## Remittance advice (`remittance_advice.csv`)

**Real-world source.** The ANSI X12 835 electronic remittance advice: a payment (BPR/TRN segments)
containing claim payments (CLP) containing service lines (SVC) with adjustments (CAS) and, at the
end, provider-level adjustments (PLB).

**Key fields.** `trace_no` and `payment_method` (ACH, CHK, NON), `payment_date`,
`file_payment_amount`, `patient_control_no` (the hospital claim number as the payer echoes it),
`payer_claim_control_no` (the payer's ICN), `claim_status_code` (1 processed, 4 denied, 22
reversal, PLB provider-level), line charge, allowed, paid, three adjustment group/reason/amount
triples, `remark_code`.

**Messiness planted.** Claim numbers padded with zeros, prefixed (PCN, CLM#), punctuated,
lower-cased or with two digits transposed; verbatim duplicate lines; reversals followed by
corrected reposts; recoupments; partial payments; late adjustments; payments split across files;
some payers echo no line control number; remittance files withheld so that money arrives without
paperwork.

**Becomes.** `core.fact_remit_line` (one adjudicated line), `core.fact_remit_adjustment` (one CAS
adjustment, unpivoted from the three triples), `core.dim_reason_code`; payments are rebuilt as
`recon.remit_trace`.

The key thing I learned about remittances is that the bank never sees claim lines. It sees
payments. A payer bundles many claim lines into one payment with one trace number and one total,
and a lockbox deposit can even merge several payments. That is why I reconcile in two steps:
first each remittance line to its claim line, then each payment to a bank deposit. The payment
total is the bridge between the two.

## Bank statement (`bank_statement.csv`)

**Real-world source.** A bank's account statement export (in practice BAI2 or CSV), one row per
transaction in statement order with a running balance.

**Key fields.** `posting_date`, `value_date`, `transaction_type` (ACH CREDIT, LOCKBOX DEPOSIT, CARD
SETTLEMENT, INTEREST, FEE, ACH DEBIT, BOOK TRANSFER), `description` (ACH credits carry
`TRN*1*<trace>*<payer tax id>`; lockbox deposits carry `ITEMS n`), `debit`, `credit`,
`running_balance`, `bank_reference`.

**Messiness planted.** Trace numbers truncated when the payer's ACH company name is long; credits
without addenda; posting dates shifted from the payer's payment date by time zone and bank cut-off;
overlapping exports that repeat a `bank_reference` (rejected as `DUPLICATE_BANK_REFERENCE`); non-payer
credits and debits (card settlements, interest, vendor payments, transfers).

**Becomes.** `core.fact_bank_txn` with `txn_category`, the extracted trace number, the inferred payer
and the lockbox item count.

## Payer contracts (`payer_contracts.csv`)

**Real-world source.** A contract fee schedule: per payer, either a dollar rate per unit for a
procedure, or a percent of charge for ALL procedures, valid for a period, sometimes "lesser of
contract and billed charge".

**Becomes.** `core.dim_payer` and `core.dim_contract_rate`. The contract expected allowed amount per
claim line is computed in `recon.build_balances()` with a LATERAL lookup (code-specific rate first,
latest period first).

## Drug purchase invoices and dispense log

**Real-world source.** A specialty wholesaler's invoice (one line per product and lot, with expiry,
packages, billing units per package, price and terms) and the pharmacy system's dispense log (one
administration or expiry-waste event, with lot, billing units and the claim it was billed on).

**Messiness planted.** Expiry written as a date or as MM/YYYY (month end); lot missing on some
administrations; claim reference missing on some; unreadable units on a few rows (imputed from the
claim line and flagged `units_imputed`); one price change by the wholesaler.

**Becomes.** `core.fact_drug_purchase` (one row = one lot), `core.fact_drug_dispense`,
`core.dim_drug` (<!--n:public.n_drugs-->25<!--/n--> J-codes with CMS reference prices).

## Claim status history (`claim_status_history.csv`)

**Real-world source.** A clearinghouse or work-queue status feed: submitted, accepted, in review,
pended, denied (with reason), appealed (with level), paid, written off.

**Messiness planted.** Many spellings of the same status, claim ids in lower case or with a leading
space, two timestamp formats, events re-sent with a new id (rejected as `DUPLICATE_EVENT`), unknown
statuses (rejected as `UNKNOWN_STATUS`).

**Becomes.** `core.fact_claim_event`, the input of the Markov chain, the denial queue and the appeal
outcomes.

## Staging and rejects

Every raw row lands in exactly one place: its typed staging table or `stg.reject` with a reason code
and the raw record as JSON. The control `RAW_ROWS_ACCOUNTED` checks raw = staged + rejected for each
file; there are <!--n:recon.staging_rejects-->519<!--/n--> rejects in the current build. Against the generator's records, every
planted bad row was rejected and no good row was (see `results/reconciliation.json`,
`staging_rejects_vs_planted`).
